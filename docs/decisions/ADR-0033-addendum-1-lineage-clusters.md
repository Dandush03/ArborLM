## 0. Terms

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Shape | One registered generator function with one question form (ADR-0033 calls it a "rule shape") |
| Lineage (as written) | A group of shapes that an author wrote as one "hand-written base rule". Its id is the registry id `<evaluation>.<name>` |
| Procedure | The algorithm a solver must carry out on an item's content to obtain the answer. (Line 419 of ADR-0033 uses "procedure" in another sense, an administrative routine; section 11) |
| Cluster | A set of lineages that share one procedure under the test of section 1. The unit for G, for every interval and for the partition |
| Global cluster | A cluster identity that holds across the four private evaluations R1, R2, M1 and C1 (section 3) |
| Map of record | The file that assigns every written lineage to a global cluster, fixed in G-gen's record of record |
| Part A, part B | The teaching-eligible and the held-out parts of an evaluation (ADR-0033 section 6.4) |
| Candidate | A written lineage that meets every condition of registration that does not depend on G or on the parts (section 4A, stage 2) |
| N_e | The number of global clusters with at least one candidate lineage in evaluation e, **both parts together**. Used only to set the split of section 4 |
| G_e,A, G_e,B; G | The number of clusters with at least one **registered** lineage of evaluation e in part A, in part B. Where one part is meant, G. This is the G of every interval, of the power rule and of the capacity demand. (Draft 2 used "G_e" for both this and N_e; review 2, F4) |
| NOT REGISTERED | A written lineage that enters no pool (section 8) |
| Cheap rule, tell | A rule that does not carry out the procedure and gets the answer right, or rules answers out, more often than the base rate |
| τ | The between-cluster standard deviation of a paired difference (ADR-0033 section 8) |

## 1. Answer 1: "distinct solution procedure" is read strictly

**Amended sentence** (section 6.2, line 268): "Each rule shape is registered with a lineage: the hand-written base rule, a distinct solution procedure, from which it derives. Shapes made mechanically from one base rule (by changing parameters, order, negation, names or surface frame) share a lineage. **The cluster, for G and for every bootstrap, is the lineage.** G is the number of lineages per part."

**Replacement reading.**

1. **The test (the drafter's text; not the human lead's words and not the auditors' rule; section 13 item 4).** Two lineages are one cluster when one algorithm, written once, solves every item of both after any combination of: renaming entities; changing the setting or surface frame; changing the order of presentation; changing parameter values (sizes, depths, number systems, moduli, tie-break rules, which position or extremum is asked); negating or inverting the question; or asking for a different read-out of the same computed state. Two lineages are distinct only if the algorithm, the data structure it maintains, or the dependency structure of its steps differs. A difference of topic, vocabulary, answer type or evaluation does not make two lineages distinct. The answer he adopted read "strict, using the auditors' maps". The auditors made their maps by their own judgement before this sentence existed. **If this test is approved, the auditor who makes the map of record re-reads every pair judgement of the working maps against it and records, per pair, "unchanged" or "changed" with the reason.** If it is not approved, this item is a description and the auditors' recorded judgement is the rule.
2. **The unit.** "The cluster, for G and for every bootstrap, is the lineage" is read as: the cluster is the set of written lineages that are one procedure under item 1. G is the number of clusters per part that have at least one registered lineage. This is the merge that line 270 already prescribes ("Such a pair is merged into one lineage before the record"), applied to every pair, not to a sample.
3. **Who decides.** The map is made by an `arbor-auditor` context that wrote none of the lineages, from code and rendered items, with its judgement hashed before it reads any author's notes. **The auditor's map governs.** Where an author disagrees with a merge, the disagreement is written beside the map with its reasons; it does not un-merge. Where the auditor is unsure, the pair is listed as borderline and is not merged; borderline pairs are reported with the count they would give if merged ("strictest"). Over-counting clusters is the harmful error, so a pair the auditor judges the same is merged even if every author disagrees. Leaving borderline pairs unmerged sits uneasily with that sentence (review note N4); printing the strictest count beside every G is the mitigation, and section 13 item 9 puts the alternative.
4. **The map of record** is the auditor's map after the narrow round of the human lead's fifth decision (section 7), completed by the cross-evaluation step of section 3. **No count quoted so far is a registered number.** The latest working counts are in `LEAD_RULINGS_4.md`: clusters left at a bound of 0.15, cheap rules only, before the cross-evaluation rule, before capacity and before the narrow round: 10 (R1), 20 (R2), 18 (M1), 17 (C1; 15 without two variants). Half of each is about 5 to 10 clusters per part. Draft 1 used 11 to 19 per part, from counts made before the bound (27, 38, 22 and 28 or 29: `LEAD_RULINGS_2.md` gives 28 for C1 as the auditor's count; `LEAD_RULINGS_3.md` gives "28 (29)" with the 29 under a column headed "(map)"; `HUMAN_LEAD_WORDS_3.md` calls 27 / 38 / 22 / 29 "the auditors' maps". The records do not say whose map the 29 is, and draft 2 was wrong to call it the implementer's. Neither figure is used here). Section 6 therefore covers G from 5 to 19.
5. **The check of line 270** ("The auditor reads one rendered item from each of 50 random pairs of lineages in one family and records any pair that is the same procedure") is replaced by: the auditor writes one line stating the procedure of every cluster; judges every pair of written lineages within an evaluation, across families and across parts; and judges every pair of clusters of two different evaluations against those statements and rendered items. Linked and borderline pairs are listed with reasons; a pair not listed is judged distinct. The 50-pair sample is no longer the check.

## 2. Answer 2: what a code-tracing item's procedure is

**Amended text.** Section 6.2, the C1 row (line 264), and section 6.3 (line 283: "C1 measures reading and tracing code. It does not measure writing it"). Neither says what a C1 item's "solution procedure" is.

**Replacement reading.**

1. For C1 the procedure of a lineage is **the algorithm of the program the item shows**, not the act of stepping through statements. (Under the other reading every C1 item is one procedure and C1 has one cluster.) The test of section 1 applies to the programs' algorithms.
2. **A lineage is admissible only if the printed line cannot be obtained without following the program's state.** If a closed formula or a library call on the program's literals gives the printed line on every item of a shape, that shape is NOT REGISTERED. Draft 1 allowed a replacement "traced-state shape". The third audits found three of the four drafted replacements answerable by closed form (lead ruling R-14), and the fifth decision allows no new lineage and no replacement shape (R-25). So in this round a lineage with a closed form is dropped, not replaced. A closed form is a cheap rule and falls under the bound of section 7.
3. A lineage for which no admissible shape exists is NOT REGISTERED.
4. Section 6.3 is otherwise unchanged. C1 is still called code tracing, never coding.

## 3. Answer 3: one algorithm, one part, across evaluations

ADR-0033 does not address the same algorithm appearing in two evaluations. **New rule, added to section 6.4 after item 1 (line 300).**

1. **Global cluster identity.** The four per-evaluation maps are joined into one graph whose nodes are all written lineages of R1, R2, M1 and C1, registered or not. Two nodes are linked if the auditor judges them one procedure under section 1, whether they are in the same evaluation or not. A C1 lineage and a lineage of another evaluation are linked when the C1 program's algorithm is the other lineage's procedure. **A global cluster is a connected component of this graph.** Its identity is the smallest lineage id it contains, in Unicode code-point order of the id strings. Links the auditor judges weaker than "the same procedure" are listed as borderline and do not join components. **A lineage that is not registered still joins.** So a lineage dropped for a tell, in another evaluation, can join two registered lineages of e into one cluster and lower e's count (test vector V4). That follows from sameness being a property of the procedure and not of a lineage's status, and it means the link judgements must be made with the same care for dropped lineages as for kept ones.
2. **Transitive merging inside an evaluation. This is a new rule. It is not in answer 3, which constrained parts only, and it lowers G (section 13 item 5).** Sameness is transitive. If a component joins two lineages of one evaluation that that evaluation's own map kept apart, they are one cluster of that evaluation, and its G falls by one. The auditor may instead withdraw a named link, with written reasons, before the map of record is fixed. Nobody else may. The alternative, in which such lineages stay two clusters and only share a part, is put in section 13.
3. **The rule.** Every global cluster with at least one candidate lineage (section 4A) is assigned to exactly one part, A or B, for all four evaluations at once (section 4), and keeps it whether or not its lineages are registered in the end. No lineage of a part-B global cluster, in any evaluation, registered or not, and no item, template or program generated by, derived from or written to imitate a lineage of a part-B global cluster, may ever be used for teaching. This replaces "no teaching item, template or family of part B" in line 300 and "no teaching code may use part-B families, checked in M1.5 by template signature" in line 419: the check in M1.5 is by global cluster as well as by template signature. Extending the ban to unregistered lineages goes beyond answer 3 and is the drafter's (section 15, A9).
4. **What the ban does not settle.** Whether teaching material from other sources that exercises a part-B procedure is excluded (teacher-written or distilled data will contain sorting, base conversion, percentages), and how M1.5 checks it, is settled in M1.5's specification. It is not settled here: read literally over every source it could make M1.5 impossible, and read loosely it could not be enforced.
5. **K0.** K0 has one part and is not a node of the graph. Its generator is "the look-up teaching material M1.5 and M2 will need" (line 326). If a K0 generator implements a part-B procedure the auditor lists it and the human lead decides; until he does, that K0 generator is not used for teaching.
6. **The map** is one file, `studies/m0/manifests/cross_evaluation_procedures.json`, frozen at F by hash. It lists every lineage, its evaluation, its global cluster, every link with the auditor's reason, every borderline pair, and the registration status of each lineage. It does not exist yet (lead ruling R-15).
7. **Limit, stated.** The rule covers the four private evaluations. Public sets (GSM8K, BIG-Bench Hard, CRUXEval, L1, K1, K2) may contain a part-B procedure; whether they count as in-teaching is settled in M1.5 (ADR-0033 section 6.4 item 3), unchanged.

## 4. Answer 4: parts A and B are assigned by shuffling clusters

**Amended sentence** (section 6.4, line 300): "**The family partition.** At the freeze, a registered seed splits the families of each private evaluation into **part A** (teaching may draw on these families) and **part B** (held out: no teaching item, template or family of part B may ever be used for teaching). Half each. Fixed before any score, so that no result can shape it."

**Replacement reading: the cluster partition.**

**The mechanics, complete (review 1, B1).** ADR-0033 line 576 says only "SHA-256 of the seed with a text label". For this role the derivation is:

- `stream(seed, l₁, …, l_k)` is `random.Random(int.from_bytes(hashlib.sha256('|'.join([str(seed), l₁, …, l_k]).encode('utf-8')).digest()[:8], 'big'))` in CPython 3.12. In words: the decimal text of the seed and the labels, joined by the single character `|` (U+007C) with nothing before or after; encoded as UTF-8; hashed with SHA-256; the first 8 bytes of the digest read as an unsigned big-endian integer; that integer seeds CPython 3.12's `random.Random` (Mersenne Twister, MT19937).
- The seed is 400004 (ADR-0033 section 12, line 562). The labels are the ASCII strings `cluster-partition` followed by `cross`, `R1`, `R2`, `M1` or `C1`.
- **To shuffle a set of clusters** is: write their identities as a list sorted in increasing Unicode code-point order of the identity strings (Python `sorted` on `str`), then call the stream's `shuffle` method on that list once. That method is the loop of CPython 3.12's `Lib/random.py`: for i from len − 1 down to 1, j = `_randbelow(i + 1)`, swap elements i and j. Each stream is created fresh and used for that one call only.
- Lineage ids are the registry ids `<evaluation>.<name>`; a cluster's identity is the smallest id it contains (section 3 item 1).
- **Test vectors** (script `partition_vector_rev3.py`, section 6's folder, SHA-256 `0036c2f9…4c0a`, output `a21e62c5…939e`; made-up identities, no cluster map read): `'400004|cluster-partition|cross'` gives the integer 3111564968117111496; the list `a` to `h`, sorted, shuffles on that stream to `e, c, b, h, d, f, g, a`. The output holds the integer and the shuffle for all five labels and five worked maps, V1 to V5. V2 to V5 exercise what draft 2's single vector did not (review 2, F19): an odd number of cross-evaluation clusters, a cluster with no candidate lineage, the clamp of step 3, an overfull part A, the case of step 3's a_e that separates the two readings (V3), and a non-candidate lineage joining two candidates (V4). Draft 2's `partition_vector.py` is kept and superseded: its step 2 started with part A. The reviewer reproduced the derived integers, the shuffles and draft 2's worked example from the text alone, byte for byte (review 2, Q2).

**The procedure**, computed by code on the map of record:

1. Only global clusters with at least one candidate lineage are assigned. Let X be those with candidate lineages in two or more evaluations, and L_e those whose candidate lineages are all in evaluation e. A lineage that is not a candidate does not make a cluster cross-evaluation and is not counted anywhere in steps 2 and 3.
2. **Cross-evaluation clusters first.** X is shuffled with `stream(400004, "cluster-partition", "cross")`. **The element at index 0 of the shuffled list goes to B, index 1 to A, index 2 to B, and so on. With an odd number B, the held-out part, has one more.** Draft 2 started with A, which gave the larger half of X to the teaching-eligible part and contradicted step 3 and the recommendation that the larger half be held out (review 2, F5). Starting with B is the drafter's choice and is listed in section 13 item 5.
3. **Then each evaluation**, for e in the order R1, R2, M1, C1. N_e is the number of global clusters with a candidate lineage in e, both parts together. a_e is the number of clusters of X **that have a candidate lineage in e** and were put in A by step 2 (a cluster of X whose only lineage in e is not a candidate is not counted; review 2, F6; vector V3 shows the two readings differ). L_e is shuffled with `stream(400004, "cluster-partition", e)`. **The split point is k_e = min(|L_e|, max(0, ⌊N_e / 2⌋ − a_e)): the elements at indices 0 to k_e − 1 of the shuffled list go to part A, the rest to part B.** With an odd N_e the held-out part B has one more. That the smaller half goes to A in both steps is the drafter's choice (the implementation's family partition did the same in its one step and flagged it for a ruling).
4. **No cluster is in both parts**, of one evaluation or of two. A test asserts it on the map of record.
5. **Unequal parts.** If a_e exceeds ⌊N_e / 2⌋, part A of e is overfull (vector V5, R2). If the X clusters of e in B exceed ⌈N_e / 2⌉, or L_e is too small to fill A (the clamp; vector V5, R1), a part is overfull in the same way. The counts are printed; nothing is moved to repair it. The first reviewer's made-up scenarios show the size of this: with an evaluation of 11 clusters of which 10 are cross-evaluation, its parts differ from the half split in 47 % of seeds and the smaller part can be forced down to **one cluster** (review 1, `partition_step5_review.out.txt`; computed under draft 1's start-with-A rule and not re-run). Section 9 item 5 says what a part of none, one or two clusters can carry.
6. **Families.** Families remain a descriptive label of a shape. They no longer define the parts, and a family may have shapes in both parts. "(strata)" is withdrawn for private evaluations if section 9 item 1 is approved. **"At least 12 families" (line 268) may not be met at the counts reached** (R1 has 10 clusters left at the bound; whether its registered lineages still span 12 families is not known), no lineage may be authored to meet it, and ADR-0033 does not say what follows if it is not met (review 2, F13). Proposed, and his to decide (section 13 item 14): the number of families among the registered lineages is printed per evaluation and per part, fewer than 12 is printed as a departure from line 268, and it stops nothing. **Until he decides, G-gen's record is not made for an evaluation whose registered lineages span fewer than 12 families.**
7. **When it is fixed (a timing change; section 15, A10).** ADR-0033 makes the partition "at the freeze" (line 300; F row, line 595). Here it is made at G-gen's record of record and hashed again at F. G-gen's record precedes G-load (line 257), so the map and the partition are fixed **after G-gen's record and before any model is run on any item**. After that record they do not change. The one exception is ADR-0033's own: the record is made again if the domain list changes or an engineering stop at the record requires it (line 587); the partition is then recomputed by the same hashed code, both partitions are kept and shown side by side, and the human lead is told. Once any model has been run on any item, nothing changes in any case.
8. **Fixed before computed (review 1, R9; review 2, F8).** Every decision on the list of section 4A stage 0 is recorded, and the partition code is written and its SHA-256 recorded, **before any partition is computed on the map of record**. The code is proposed at `studies/m0/cluster_partition.py`; its hash goes into G-gen's record. It must reproduce the five test vectors. Where the code and this text disagree it is an engineering stop, not a reading. **Exactly one partition is computed on the map of record** (section 4A); if the record is made again under item 7, every partition is kept. The reason this matters: splits of families on seed 400004 have already been seen (`studies/m0/manifests/r1_rev4_parts.md` on `claude/m0-impl`; the "about 13 / 14" figure). The new labels give streams independent of those, and neither `studies/`, `tests/` and `docs/` on `claude/m0-impl` at `9bcec484` nor that worktree's uncommitted files contain the label `cluster-partition` (the drafter's search of the commit; the second reviewer's search of both).
9. **If borderline pairs are to be kept in one part (section 13 item 9; not the drafted default).** The drafted default leaves them free and steps 1 to 3 run as written. If he chooses to keep them together, the unit of steps 1 to 3 becomes the group: the components of the graph whose links are the borderline pairs between clusters. A group's identity is the smallest cluster identity in it; it is in X if its clusters have candidate lineages in two or more evaluations; N_e, a_e, |L_e| and k_e are counted in groups; every cluster takes its group's part. G and every interval still count clusters.

Reasons for steps 2 and 3 instead of one shuffle of all global clusters: one global shuffle satisfies answers 3 and 4 but leaves each evaluation's split to chance (for 27 of 116 clusters, about 13.5 ± 2.3 in part A; reproduced by both reviewers). The two-step rule gives ⌊half⌋ per evaluation whenever step 5 does not bite. The simpler rule is an option in section 13 item 5.

## 4A. The order of record (review 2, F1)

Draft 2 defined registration, capacity, G per part and the partition in terms of each other: registration needed capacity, the capacity demand needed G per part, G per part needed the partition, and the partition needed registration. Several implementations fitted the text and registered different parts. This section gives one order. Every stage uses only the stages above it.

- **Stage 0. Decided and hashed first.** Recorded before anything below is computed on the map of record: the human lead's domain list (choice 2); the approval of this addendum; and section 13 items 1 (distinct item), 4 (same-procedure test), 5 (partition mechanics and this order), 6 (pool builder), 8 (levels for the bound), 9 (borderline pairs), 12 (the two-sided reading), 13 (no minimum), 14 (the family requirement) and 16 (the G of the capacity demand). **This is the one list of what G-gen's record waits for;** sections 4, 5 and 11 refer to it. Then the code is hashed: the cheap-rule scripts with their seeds and sample sizes, the distinct-item key, the partition code, the pool builder and the capacity runner. Section 13 items 2, 3 and 7 and the interval method of section 9 item 4 are analysis rules; they do not change which lineages are registered or how they are split, do not bind G-gen's record, and are fixed with the plan at F.
- **Stage 1. The map of record.** The auditor's judgements (re-read against the test if item 4 is approved), every link within and across evaluations over every written lineage whatever its status, the components, the transitive merging of section 3. It depends on nothing below and nothing below revises it.
- **Stage 2. Candidates.** A lineage is a candidate when every condition of registration that does not depend on G or on the parts holds and is recorded: its place in the map of record; its wording read by the auditor; no cheap rule beyond the bound at any level (section 7); levels distinct; a second solver from a separate context (section 8) that agrees with the first on every fixture; the pair audited.
- **Stage 3. The partition, once.** Section 4, on the global clusters with at least one candidate lineage.
- **Stage 4. Capacity, per evaluation part, to a fixed point.** For evaluation e and part p, start with S, the candidate lineages of e whose cluster is in p. One round: G is the number of clusters with at least one lineage in S, and k_c the number of lineages of cluster c in S. A lineage fails if at any of the seven levels its count of distinct items is below D_lin(G, k_c); a cluster fails if at any level the count of distinct items of its lineages together is below D(G), and then all its lineages fail (section 10). A lineage whose two solvers disagree on any item generated in this stage fails in the same way. All failing lineages are removed from S at once and the round is run again, until a round removes nothing. Removing a lineage can only raise the demand on the others, so the result does not depend on the order in which failures are found: it is the largest set of lineages that meets its own demand. Counts are made by generation with the builder's own drawing routine and seeded streams; a later round's higher demand extends the same generation. The eight evaluation parts are independent of each other in this stage.
- **Stage 5. Registration.** The lineages left in S are registered. G_e,A and G_e,B are the numbers of clusters with a registered lineage. The family count of section 4 item 6 is read here.
- **Stage 6. G-gen's record of record.** It holds the map, the candidates, the partition and its code hash, the rounds of stage 4, the registered lineages, G per part with the strictest counts beside it, N_e, and every hash of stage 0. After it, a failure of any of these conditions is an ENGINEERING STOP (sections 7 and 10).

**What happens to the partition after a capacity drop: nothing (a new registered choice; the drafter's proposal; section 13 item 5).** A cluster keeps the part stage 3 gave it, whether or not any of its lineages survives stage 4. Nothing is shuffled again and no cluster is moved to rebalance. Parts may end unequal, or empty; the counts are printed. The alternatives are to compute the partition again after every drop, or to iterate partition and capacity together until neither changes. Reasons for keeping it:

1. Exactly one partition is ever computed on the map of record with seed 400004. There is no second split to compare with the first and so nothing to choose between.
2. A cluster's part is then a function of the map and the candidates only, both fixed before any capacity is measured. The teaching ban on a dropped lineage (section 8) refers to a part that cannot move.
3. Under either alternative the split would depend on capacity counts, and the capacity demand depends on the split. Computing again after a drop reshuffles every remaining cluster, which can make a different lineage fail; nothing guarantees that such a loop settles, and a rule for stopping it would be one more registered choice.
4. The cost is imbalance between the parts, which step 5 of section 4 already allows and prints.

**Why the partition comes before capacity, and not capacity at a G fixed in advance.** The second reviewer's example was to compute capacity at a G fixed in advance, register, then partition once. The cross-evaluation rule can leave a part smaller than any G fixed in advance (section 4 step 5), so that gate would have to be read again after the partition, which is the circle again. Putting the partition first removes it.

A worked example on made-up capacities is in `partition_vector_rev3.out.txt`, part 4: one part falls from 3 clusters to 1 in three rounds (demand per cluster 81,010, then 121,000, then 241,000), another ends empty, and no cluster changes part.

   **D(G) = 10 × (⌈4,000 / G⌉ + ⌈20,000 / G⌉ + 100)** distinct items per cluster.

   4,000 is the development pool, 20,000 the sealed pool, 100 the tuning draw per level and part (section 8, lines 387 to 390). **The two reserve levels carry the full demand, not 10 × 100:** section 6.5 takes as candidates "every contiguous range of levels" and, if no range is interior, scores the reserve levels "and the rule is applied again", so a reserve level can carry the registered level mix and the pools with it. Taking the 100 tuning items whole for one cluster is more than the worst case (review note N8); it is kept because it is harmless and simpler. **G is that part's own count, G_e,A or G_e,B, in the current round of section 4A stage 4.** Lead ruling R-11 took "the smaller part's cluster count" for both parts; this text takes each part's own, which for the larger part is **less strict** than R-11 (review 2, F11). The reason: line 273 counts "the number the pools demand", and a part's pools are spread over that part's clusters, so the larger part's clusters are asked for fewer items. It is a departure from the lead's own ruling, stated in sections 12 and 15 and listed in section 13 item 16.
3. **Values** (script part A): D(5) = 49,000; D(8) = 31,000; D(10) = 25,000; D(11) = 22,830; D(13) = 19,470; D(14) = 18,150; D(15) = 17,010; D(19) = 13,640. At the withdrawn G = 300 it was 1,810. A cluster must hold about seven and a half to twenty-seven times what it had to under the design as approved, on every one of seven levels. The sealed pool may be raised later, never lowered (line 409); a raise re-runs this gate.
4. **Per cluster and per lineage.** The gate is read on the cluster: the number of distinct items its registered lineages produce together at that level, counted on items generated by the builder of part B and not on a combinatorial argument. Because part B spreads a cluster's quota evenly over its registered lineages, each registered lineage of a cluster of k must also hold 10 times its own largest share: **D_lin(G, k) = 10 × (⌈⌈4,000/G⌉ / k⌉ + ⌈⌈20,000/G⌉ / k⌉ + ⌈100 / k⌉)**. For example D_lin(10, 2) = 12,500 and D_lin(13, 3) = 6,500. A lineage that cannot is NOT REGISTERED, and the gate is read again by the rounds of section 4A stage 4, with k and G as they then stand, until nothing more fails. The partition is not computed again. At the smallest counts the fixed point can reach, the demand is D(4) = 61,000, D(3) = 81,010, D(2) = 121,000 and D(1) = 241,000 (`addendum1_rev3_arithmetic.out.txt`).
5. **No cluster at some levels only.** A cluster that fails at any of the seven levels is not registered at all: registering it at some levels would make G differ by level, against the even spread of section 8 and the level rule of section 6.5. Lead ruling R-5 allowed "NOT REGISTERED at that level set"; this text refuses it and says so (the drafter's choice; conservative; reported to the human lead).
6. **The event is unchanged for the record of record:** a capacity failure there is an ENGINEERING STOP (section 10.7). Before the record, dropping a lineage is ordinary work under section 7 item 5, reported to the human lead with the list.

### Part B. The pool builder (a proposal; needs approval; section 13 item 6)

**The builder as written does not do what this addendum needs.** `studies/m0/core.py` on `claude/m0-impl` (`generate_pool`, read at `9bcec484`): it takes the lineages of a part from the family partition, gives every **lineage** an equal quota (`allocate(size, [1.0] * len(lins))`), splits each lineage's quota over levels by the level mix, draws with `stream(seed, role, salt_label, eval_id, part, lin.id, str(lv))`, and rejects an item whose normalised text has been used (`text_hash`: NFKC, case-folded, whitespace collapsed), so two items that differ only in names are two items. `m0_draw` balances the 1,500 over lineages in the same way. With lineages merged into clusters this is no longer "balanced over the G clusters" (line 388) or "spread evenly over the G clusters" (line 390): on a made-up part of 7 clusters holding 3, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 lineages the largest cluster would get 30 % of every pool instead of one seventh (script part E), and the effective number of clusters would be below G.

**Proposed builder.** For evaluation e, part p, a role, its seed, a size and a level mix, on the map of record:

1. **Clusters.** The global clusters assigned to p that have at least one registered lineage of e (in the capacity stage: at least one lineage still in S), in increasing code-point order of identity.
2. **Cluster quotas.** The size is divided equally over those clusters by largest remainder, ties to the cluster earlier in that order (`allocate` as written).
3. **Levels inside a cluster.** The cluster's quota is divided over the levels of the mix, in increasing level order, by largest remainder with the mix as weights.
4. **Lineages inside a cluster and level.** That count is divided equally over the cluster's registered lineages of e, in increasing order of id, by largest remainder. A lineage draws its shapes as its generator already does.
5. **Streams.** Unchanged: `stream(seed, role, salt_label, e, p, lineage id, level)`, with the derivation of section 4. A lineage's stream therefore does not depend on which cluster it sits in.
6. **Uniqueness and order.** Unchanged: an item is rejected if its normalised text is already used in any earlier role or earlier in this pool. Generation runs over clusters, then levels, then lineages, in the orders above, so the shared set of used texts is deterministic. A quota a lineage cannot fill is a capacity failure and is never moved to a sibling lineage or cluster.
7. **The M0 draw.** The 1,500 are divided equally over the part's clusters, then equally over each cluster's registered lineages, by largest remainder in the same orders; each lineage's items are sampled from its development items with `stream(400006, "m0-draw", lineage id)` as written.
8. **The record.** Every item carries its global cluster identity; pool hashes, counts "per family, cluster and level" (line 419) and every analysis use that field. The clusters of every interval and of the power simulation are these clusters, not lineages.

**Capacity is judged against a specified builder, never an assumed one (lead rulings R-22, R-28), and no capacity run of record takes place before section 13 items 1 and 6 are decided (review 2, F2).** Draft 2 said that until this part is approved capacity is computed against `generate_pool` as written. That cannot be done under the cluster partition: `generate_pool` takes a part's lineages from `part_lineages`, which calls `family_partition`, the withdrawn split of families on the label `family-partition`. The capacity figures produced so far, in the narrow round under R-28, are working figures on that footing and are not of record; R-28's rule for them stands (a lineage that passes only under a per-cluster builder is reported as such and is not counted as passing).

- **If he approves the per-cluster builder (item 6 a):** capacity is computed against it in section 4A stage 4, with D(G) and D_lin(G, k) of part A, and its code is hashed at stage 0.
- **If he keeps the builder as written (item 6 b):** it is changed in one respect only, which it cannot run without: a part's lineages are those whose global cluster section 4 assigned to that part. It still gives every lineage an equal quota. The demand is then per lineage and level, **D_w(L) = 10 × (⌈4,000 / L⌉ + ⌈20,000 / L⌉ + 100)**, where L is the number of lineages of e in the part in the current round; for example D_w(8) = 31,000, D_w(15) = 17,010, D_w(30) = 9,010 (`addendum1_rev3_arithmetic.out.txt`). Stage 4 runs with L in place of G and k, and there is no cluster-level condition. A cluster of k lineages then holds k / L of every pool and not 1 / G (for example 0.300 against 0.143), G for every interval still counts clusters, and lines 388 and 390 are not met as worded.

Draft 1's sentence that items are spread "inside a lineage over its shapes" is withdrawn: a lineage's generator already chooses among its shapes, and this addendum adds no rule there.
