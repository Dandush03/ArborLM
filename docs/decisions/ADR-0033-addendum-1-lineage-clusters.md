## 0. Terms

| Procedure | The algorithm a solver must carry out on an item's content to obtain the answer. (Line 419 of ADR-0033 uses "procedure" in another sense, an administrative routine; section 11) |

## 1. Answer 1: "distinct solution procedure" is read strictly

**Amended sentence** (section 6.2, line 268): "Each rule shape is registered with a lineage: the hand-written base rule, a distinct solution procedure, from which it derives. Shapes made mechanically from one base rule (by changing parameters, order, negation, names or surface frame) share a lineage. **The cluster, for G and for every bootstrap, is the lineage.** G is the number of lineages per part."

**Replacement reading.**

1. **The test (the drafter's text, not the auditors' rule; approved in principle by the human lead, 2026-11-26, section 13 item 4 (a); it takes effect with the approval of this addendum).** Two lineages are one cluster when one algorithm, written once, solves every item of both after any combination of: renaming entities; changing the setting or surface frame; changing the order of presentation; changing parameter values (sizes, depths, number systems, moduli, tie-break rules, which position or extremum is asked); negating or inverting the question; or asking for a different read-out of the same computed state. Two lineages are distinct only if the algorithm, the data structure it maintains, or the dependency structure of its steps differs. A difference of topic, vocabulary, answer type or evaluation does not make two lineages distinct. The answer he adopted read "strict, using the auditors' maps". The auditors made their maps by their own judgement before this sentence existed. **The auditor who makes the map of record re-reads every pair judgement of the working maps against the test and records, per pair, "unchanged" or "changed" with the reason.**
2. **The unit.** "The cluster, for G and for every bootstrap, is the lineage" is read as: the cluster is the set of written lineages that are one procedure under item 1. G is the number of clusters per part that have at least one registered lineage. This is the merge that line 270 already prescribes ("Such a pair is merged into one lineage before the record"), applied to every pair, not to a sample.
3. **Who decides.** The map is made by an `arbor-auditor` context that wrote none of the lineages, from code and rendered items, with its judgement hashed before it reads any author's notes. **The auditor's map governs.** Where an author disagrees with a merge, the disagreement is written beside the map with its reasons; it does not un-merge. Where the auditor is unsure, the pair is listed as borderline and is not merged; borderline pairs are reported with the count they would give if merged ("strictest"). Over-counting clusters is the harmful error, so a pair the auditor judges the same is merged even if every author disagrees. Leaving borderline pairs unmerged sits uneasily with that sentence (review note N4). Two things answer it: the strictest count is printed beside every G, and a borderline pair is kept in one part by the partition (decided, section 13 item 9; mechanics in section 4).
4. **The map of record** is the auditor's map after the narrow round of the human lead's fifth decision (section 7), completed by the cross-evaluation step of section 3. **No count quoted so far is a registered number.** The latest working counts are in `LEAD_RULINGS_7.md` (R-38, R-42), after the narrow round and the independent final checks, under the counting rule of section 7 items 2 and 4 (decided, section 13 item 18), before the membership audit of section 4A stage 0, before the map of record and before capacity of record: R1 10 lineages in 8 clusters; R2 16 in 16 (one more lineage held on capacity); M1 29 in 17 (eight lineages held on capacity; 16 clusters if they go); C1 26 in 17 (15 without two variants). With the strong cross-evaluation links only, the parts would be 4 and 4 (R1), 8 and 8 (R2), 8 and 9 (M1), 8 and 9 (C1). With the borderline pairs taken together the auditor's recomputation, which counted in groups, gave 2 or 3 clusters for R1's smaller part and 8 for the others; it has not been recomputed under the placement rule of section 4 (decided, section 13 item 20), and no split of the real clusters with the registered seed has been computed (R-42). Under the option not taken for item 18 (every class of rule counts) the counts would be lower: R1 6 clusters or fewer, R2 about ten lineages fewer, C1 15 clusters (R-38; the record gives no figure for M1). So a part is expected to hold about 2 to 9 clusters. Draft 3 gave 10, 20, 18 and 17 from `LEAD_RULINGS_4.md`, about 5 to 10 per part. Draft 1 used 11 to 19 per part, from counts made before the bound (27, 38, 22 and 28 or 29: `LEAD_RULINGS_2.md` gives 28 for C1 as the auditor's count; `LEAD_RULINGS_3.md` gives "28 (29)" with the 29 under a column headed "(map)"; `HUMAN_LEAD_WORDS_3.md` calls 27 / 38 / 22 / 29 "the auditors' maps". The records do not say whose map the 29 is, and draft 2 was wrong to call it the implementer's. Neither figure is used here). Section 6 covers G from 5 to 19 in its tables and G of 2, 3 and 4 in a paragraph under them.
5. **The check of line 270** ("The auditor reads one rendered item from each of 50 random pairs of lineages in one family and records any pair that is the same procedure") is replaced by: the auditor writes one line stating the procedure of every cluster; judges every pair of written lineages within an evaluation, across families and across parts; and judges every pair of clusters of two different evaluations against those statements and rendered items. Linked and borderline pairs are listed with reasons; a pair not listed is judged distinct. The 50-pair sample is no longer the check.

## 3. Answer 3: one algorithm, one part, across evaluations

ADR-0033 does not address the same algorithm appearing in two evaluations. **New rule, added to section 6.4 after item 1 (line 300).**

1. **Global cluster identity.** The four per-evaluation maps are joined into one graph whose nodes are all written lineages of R1, R2, M1 and C1, registered or not. Two nodes are linked if the auditor judges them one procedure under section 1, whether they are in the same evaluation or not. A C1 lineage and a lineage of another evaluation are linked when the C1 program's algorithm is the other lineage's procedure. **A global cluster is a connected component of this graph.** Its identity is the smallest lineage id it contains, in Unicode code-point order of the id strings. Links the auditor judges weaker than "the same procedure" are listed as borderline and do not join components; they join groups, which the partition keeps in one part (section 4). **A lineage that is not registered still joins.** So a lineage dropped for a tell, in another evaluation, can join two registered lineages of e into one cluster and lower e's count (test vector V4). That follows from sameness being a property of the procedure and not of a lineage's status, and it means the link judgements must be made with the same care for dropped lineages as for kept ones.
2. **Transitive merging inside an evaluation. This is a new rule. It is not in answer 3, which constrained parts only, and it lowers G. Decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 5 (iv)).** Sameness is transitive. If a component joins two lineages of one evaluation that that evaluation's own map kept apart, they are one cluster of that evaluation, and its G falls by one. The auditor may instead withdraw a named link, with written reasons, before the map of record is hashed (section 4A stage 1). Nobody else may, and nobody may after that hash. The alternative, in which such lineages stay two clusters and only share a part, was not taken.
3. **The rule.** Every global cluster with at least one candidate lineage (section 4A) is assigned to exactly one part, A or B, for all four evaluations at once (section 4), and keeps it whether or not its lineages are registered in the end. No lineage of a part-B global cluster, in any evaluation, registered or not, and no item, template or program generated by, derived from or written to imitate a lineage of a part-B global cluster, may ever be used for teaching. This replaces "no teaching item, template or family of part B" in line 300 and "no teaching code may use part-B families, checked in M1.5 by template signature" in line 419: the check in M1.5 is by global cluster as well as by template signature. Extending the ban to unregistered lineages goes beyond answer 3 and is the drafter's (section 15, A9).
4. **What the ban does not settle.** Whether teaching material from other sources that exercises a part-B procedure is excluded (teacher-written or distilled data will contain sorting, base conversion, percentages), and how M1.5 checks it, is settled in M1.5's specification. It is not settled here: read literally over every source it could make M1.5 impossible, and read loosely it could not be enforced.
5. **K0.** K0 has one part and is not a node of the graph. Its generator is "the look-up teaching material M1.5 and M2 will need" (line 326). If a K0 generator implements a part-B procedure the auditor lists it and the human lead decides; until he does, that K0 generator is not used for teaching.
6. **The map** is one file, `studies/m0/manifests/cross_evaluation_procedures.json`, frozen at F by hash. It lists every lineage, its evaluation, its global cluster, every link with the auditor's reason, every borderline pair, and the registration status of each lineage. It does not exist yet (lead ruling R-15).
7. **Limit, stated.** The rule covers the four private evaluations. Public sets (GSM8K, BIG-Bench Hard, CRUXEval, L1, K1, K2) may contain a part-B procedure; whether they count as in-teaching is settled in M1.5 (ADR-0033 section 6.4 item 3), unchanged.

## 4. Answer 4: parts A and B are assigned by shuffling clusters

**Amended sentence** (section 6.4, line 300): "**The family partition.** At the freeze, a registered seed splits the families of each private evaluation into **part A** (teaching may draw on these families) and **part B** (held out: no teaching item, template or family of part B may ever be used for teaching). Half each. Fixed before any score, so that no result can shape it."

**Replacement reading: the cluster partition.**

**The mechanics, complete (review 1, B1).** ADR-0033 line 576 says only "SHA-256 of the seed with a text label". For this role the derivation is:

- `stream(seed, l₁, …, l_k)` is `random.Random(int.from_bytes(hashlib.sha256('|'.join([str(seed), l₁, …, l_k]).encode('utf-8')).digest()[:8], 'big'))` in CPython 3.12. In words: the decimal text of the seed and the labels, joined by the single character `|` (U+007C) with nothing before or after; encoded as UTF-8; hashed with SHA-256; the first 8 bytes of the digest read as an unsigned big-endian integer; that integer seeds CPython 3.12's `random.Random` (Mersenne Twister, MT19937).
- The seed is 400004 (ADR-0033 section 12, line 562). The labels are the ASCII strings `cluster-partition` followed by `cross`, `R1`, `R2`, `M1` or `C1`.
- **To shuffle a set of clusters** is: write their identities as a list sorted in increasing Unicode code-point order of the identity strings (Python `sorted` on `str`), then call the stream's `shuffle` method on that list once. That method is the loop of CPython 3.12's `Lib/random.py`: for i from len − 1 down to 1, j = `_randbelow(i + 1)`, swap elements i and j. Each stream is created fresh and used for that one call only.
- Lineage ids are the registry ids `<evaluation>.<name>`; a cluster's identity is the smallest id it contains (section 3 item 1).
- **Test vectors** (scripts in section 6's folder; made-up identities that name no written lineage; no cluster map read). `partition_vector_rev3.py` (SHA-256 `0036c2f9…4c0a`, output `a21e62c5…939e`): `'400004|cluster-partition|cross'` gives the integer 3111564968117111496; the list `a` to `h`, sorted, shuffles on that stream to `e, c, b, h, d, f, g, a`. Its output holds the integer and the shuffle for all five labels and five worked maps, V1 to V5, none with a borderline pair. V2 to V5 exercise what draft 2's single vector did not (review 2, F19): an odd number of cross-evaluation clusters, a cluster with no candidate lineage, the clamp of step 3, an overfull part A, the case of step 3's a_e that separates the two readings (V3), and a non-candidate lineage joining two candidates (V4). `partition_vector_rev4.py` (`3b28dbbb…527f`, output `c4fbae36…ec8d`), new in draft 4, implements steps 1 to 3 as written below. It reproduces V1 to V5 unchanged (asserted in the script, under every reading it computes) and adds three vectors with borderline pairs (review 3, G4): V6 has a group that spans a cross-evaluation cluster, holds a cluster with no candidate lineage and holds two clusters of one evaluation; V7 and V8 are the third reviewer's probes B2 and B3. Draft 2's `partition_vector.py` is kept and superseded: its step 2 started with part A. The second reviewer reproduced the derived integers, the shuffles and draft 2's worked example from the text alone, byte for byte (review 2, Q2); the third reproduced V1 to V5 of draft 3 from the text alone (review 3). The fourth checker reproduced V1 to V8 with code of his own, under every option the text then listed, and found no disagreement with the drafter's code on 4,000 made-up random maps (check 4, scope 2). He read the drafter's script before writing his own and copied the maps of V6 to V8 from it, so that check is not a blind one from the text.

**The procedure**, computed by code on the map of record. The unit that is placed is the group (section 0). Balance is counted in clusters. With no borderline pair every group is one cluster, and steps 1 to 3 are those of draft 3 word for word in their effect.

1. **What is assigned.** The groups are the connected components of the graph whose nodes are the global clusters and whose links are the borderline pairs between two different clusters. **A cluster with no candidate lineage is a node of that graph: it passes a group on, and it takes no part (section 8). Decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 17).** Under the option not taken such a cluster is not a node and a borderline pair that touches it does nothing; vectors V6 and V7 show that the two give different parts. Only groups in which at least one cluster has a candidate lineage are assigned. A group's identity is the smallest identity among its clusters that have a candidate lineage. For a group g, w(g) is the number of its clusters with a candidate lineage, and w_e(g) the number of its clusters with a candidate lineage in evaluation e. Let X be the groups whose clusters have candidate lineages in two or more evaluations, and L_e the groups whose candidate lineages are all in evaluation e. A lineage that is not a candidate does not make a group cross-evaluation and is not counted in w, w_e or N_e.
2. **Cross-evaluation groups first.** X is shuffled with `stream(400004, "cluster-partition", "cross")`. The shuffled list is walked from index 0. **A group goes to part B if part B so far holds no more clusters than part A, and otherwise to part A**, where "holds" counts w(g) over the groups already placed in this step. With no borderline pair this is draft 3's rule exactly: index 0 to B, index 1 to A, index 2 to B, and so on; with an odd number B, the held-out part, has one more in this step. That the walk starts with B was decided by the human lead on 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 5 (iii)); draft 2 started with A (review 2, F5). **That a group of several clusters is placed by the count of clusters was decided by him the same day (section 13 item 20).** **The held-out part usually has the larger half. It does not always (check 4, H5), and this step is the cause.** With groups of unequal size part B can end this step with fewer clusters than A (two groups of one and three clusters, in that order), and a cross-evaluation group can put more than ⌊N_e / 2⌋ of an evaluation's clusters in part A. Step 3 then compensates inside each evaluation only as far as that evaluation's own groups allow, and step 5 prints what is left. On the fourth checker's 4,000 made-up random maps, under the rule decided, part A held more clusters than part B in 1,345 of 14,091 evaluation-instances (9.5 %), and in 270 (1.9 %) with the borderline pairs removed; this step ended with fewer clusters in B than in A in 973 of the 4,000 maps with borderline pairs and in none without. In every one of the 1,345 the cause was this step (a_e above ⌊N_e / 2⌋); step 3 never took part A above ⌊N_e / 2⌋. **Those rates are indicative only:** they depend on the checker's made-up distribution of maps (`addendum1_review4/partition_check4.out.txt`), they say nothing of the real clusters, and the drafter has not re-run them.
3. **Then each evaluation**, for e in the order R1, R2, M1, C1. N_e is the number of global clusters with a candidate lineage in e, both parts together, counted in clusters. a_e is the sum of w_e(g) over the groups of X that step 2 put in part A: the number of clusters with a candidate lineage in e that are already in A (a cluster of X whose only lineage in e is not a candidate is not counted; review 2, F6; vector V3). L_e is shuffled with `stream(400004, "cluster-partition", e)` and walked from index 0, with p the number of clusters this step has so far put in part A. **A group g goes to part A if a_e + p + w_e(g) ≤ ⌊N_e / 2⌋, and otherwise to part B.** With no borderline pair this is draft 3's split point k_e = min(|L_e|, max(0, ⌊N_e / 2⌋ − a_e)): the elements at indices 0 to k_e − 1 go to part A and the rest to part B. This step never takes part A above ⌊N_e / 2⌋ clusters, so with an odd N_e the held-out part B has at least one more, unless step 2 has already put more than ⌊N_e / 2⌋ of e's clusters in part A (step 5). That the smaller half goes to A was decided by the human lead on 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 5 (ii)). **That a group which does not fit is passed over and the walk continues was decided by him on 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 20).** The two rules not taken are computed beside it on V6 and V8 (`partition_vector_rev4.out.txt`, part 3c): counted in groups, as draft 3's item 9 read and as his own item 9 therefore referenced, part A of V8 gets four of six clusters, against his item 5; stopping at the first group that does not fit, it gets one of six.
4. **Every cluster with a candidate lineage takes its group's part. No cluster is in both parts**, of one evaluation or of two, and no group is. A test asserts both on the map of record. A cluster with no candidate lineage has no part, whatever group it sits in; the part of its group is printed beside it for M1.5, which settles whether teaching may draw on it (section 8).
5. **Unequal parts.** If a_e exceeds ⌊N_e / 2⌋, part A of e is overfull (vector V5, R2). If the clusters of X in B with a candidate lineage in e exceed ⌈N_e / 2⌉, or L_e cannot fill A (the clamp; vector V5, R1), or the groups of L_e are too large to fit (vector V8 under the rule not taken), a part is larger than its half in the same way. The counts are printed in clusters and in groups; nothing is moved to repair it. The first reviewer's made-up scenarios show the size of this: with an evaluation of 11 clusters of which 10 are cross-evaluation, its parts differ from the half split in 47 % of seeds and the smaller part can be forced down to **one cluster** (review 1, `partition_step5_review.out.txt`; computed under draft 1's start-with-A rule and not re-run). 2. A cluster's part is then a function of the map and the candidates only, both hashed before any capacity is measured. The teaching ban on a dropped lineage (section 8) refers to a part that cannot move.

It was named to him as such, and it was taken with the counts under both options in view.** Counted: the cheap rules, that is the classes the fourth-audit auditor of each evaluation counted in its "bound 0.15" column (R-31), in the union of the implementer's battery and the auditors' rule scripts; every closed form for a C1 program (section 2); and every cheap rule or closed form the final-check auditors newly found (R-38). Measured, reported per lineage and **not counted**: rules the auditor classed as the correct procedure, as costly, as a partial run of the procedure (partial trace), or as an artefact of the measurement. The class of a rule is the auditor's: where a pooling script prints an auditor's reading and an implementer's side by side, the auditor's is the one of record. Draft 3 said here "No class of rule is exempt … every rule in the scripts counts". That was R-24 as the drafter read it, it was never the rule applied, and the two differ in outcome: R-38 records that if the uncounted classes counted, R1 would fall to 6 clusters or fewer, R2 would lose about ten lineages and C1 would fall to 15 clusters (R2 `crt_smallest`, whose truncated search scores 0.37 and is classed partial, is one that stays). **The rule decided is the less strict of the two.** The reason given to him for it: the table he chose 0.15 from was the cheap-rule table (`LEAD_RULINGS_4.md`: "cheap rules only"), so it is the rule his number was set against. The lineages listed in the four final reports as "would drop if those classes counted" stay, subject to every other condition.
5. **What may be repaired (R-25), and nothing else:** the two package-hygiene failures; wording defects named in the fourth audits, by changing the wording or the stated convention and never by rejecting items on their answer; collapsed levels, so that every level produces distinct items with difficulty rising by a stated parameter. Any lineage with another audited defect is dropped: a counted rule above the bound, a closed form, answer-conditional rejection not needed for the item to be well posed, a capacity failure that widening would not cure without collapsing levels. **No new lineage and no replacement shape.**
6. **No loop (R-26).** After the repairs the same scripts run again on every surviving lineage. Anything above the bound, with levels still not distinct, or failing capacity, is dropped, not repaired. There is no further repair round. That round has been run (the narrow round; `LEAD_RULINGS_6.md`, `_7.md`); its runs are among those of the manifest.
7. **Redrawing items conditional on a rule's outcome, or fitting a probability to it, is forbidden** (lead rulings R-1, R-13, R-18, R-19). These are lead rulings carried into this text, not the human lead's words; approving the addendum approves them. A rejected rule that scores exactly 0.00 is itself a tell.
8. Every convention an answer depends on is stated in the item. An item whose key changes under an ordinary alternative reading is a defect of its lineage (R-4), repaired in wording under item 5 or the lineage is NOT REGISTERED.
9. **What the gate does not show.** The bound holds only against the rules that were written, and only against those counted. In each audit round the auditors found cheap rules the implementers' batteries lacked, some created by the repairs; the final checks found three more and a closed form (R-38). A lineage that passes is one in which no counted rule exceeds 0.15; it is not shown free of tells, and it may be one that a partial run of its own procedure answers well above the base rate. Points the lead left as they are, none above the bound as item 2 applies it, are listed for the human lead in R-40 (among them within-shape rates of 0.30 and 0.17 in two C1 lineages). This is one more reason the private readings are descriptive.
10. **Before the record and at the record.** Before G-gen's record of record, a lineage beyond the bound is dropped under item 2 as ordinary work, reported to the human lead with the list. A counted rule of the hashed scripts found beyond the bound at or after the record of record is an ENGINEERING STOP (ADR-0033 section 10.7), like the other validity gates.

## 8. Second solvers, and lineages NOT REGISTERED

**Second solvers.** Line 261 ("an independent second solver re-derives it from the item's structured form") and line 275 ("The auditor context that writes the second solvers does not audit them: a separate auditor context audits the solver pair") stand and are made exact:

1. Three separate contexts. The author writes the generator and its solver. A second context, an `arbor-auditor` that has not read the generator's or the first solver's source, writes the second solver from the item's structured form, its schema and fixtures only. **The fixtures it receives carry no answer from the first solver** (review note N3). A third `arbor-auditor` context audits the pair.
2. A second solver written by the context that wrote the generator does not satisfy line 261. The implementation's own notes record that the existing second solvers were written by the implementer; they are rewritten under item 1 before registration.
3. Which model ran each context is recorded from session metadata.

**Registration.** A lineage is registered when it is a candidate (section 4A stage 2: its place in the map of record; its wording read by the auditor; no counted rule beyond the bound at any level; levels distinct; a second solver under item 1 that agrees with the first on every fixture; the pair audited) and it survives the capacity stage (section 4A stage 4), in which the two solvers must also agree on every generated item. The order is that of section 4A and no other. The solvers' agreement on every pool item is checked again at the freeze, as ADR-0033's validity gate; a disagreement there is an ENGINEERING STOP.

**NOT REGISTERED.** A written lineage that fails any condition, or is superseded, is marked NOT REGISTERED with its reason in the map of record. It is kept on file and never deleted. It is excluded everywhere: from the cluster count, from G, from fixtures, from capacity runs and from every tuning, development and sealed pool (lead ruling R-12). It stays a node of the graph of section 3, keeps its global cluster, and its cluster's part binds it under section 3 item 3. A global cluster with no candidate lineage has no part; it is listed, with the part of its group beside it if a borderline pair ties it to one (section 4 steps 1 and 4), and whether teaching may draw on it is settled in M1.5, not here. A cluster that had a candidate and lost every lineage at the capacity stage keeps its part. A cluster is not registered at some levels only: see section 10 item 5.

## 9. Statistics at fewer than 30 clusters (consequences in section 9 of ADR-0033)

No rule of section 9 is relaxed. The places below need exact text because clusters no longer sit inside families and are few.

1. **Strata (a new analysis choice; decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26, section 13 item 3 (a)).** Section 9.2 (line 435): "Cluster bootstrap, stratified by family or task". Section 9.4 (line 456) draws clusters "within strata" and defines τ̂² from "the variance of the cluster means within strata"; line 468 says the simulation keeps "the pairing, the strata and M5's own number of clusters". For private evaluation parts a cluster may now span several families, and with 5 to 19 clusters over 12 or more families most strata would hold one cluster. The implementation's reading is that a stratum with one cluster contributes nothing to the between-cluster variance; at these counts that drives τ̂ toward zero and the floor toward "met". **Replacement reading, decided: a private evaluation part is one stratum.** Families are printed as subcategories only (line 431). The reviewer checked the direction: one stratum is unfavourable to passing, not a relaxation (with 13 clusters over 12 family strata the truncation probability at τ = 0.03 is about 0.61, against about 0.29 with one stratum; it also widens every t interval). It changes an analysis rule that ADR-0032 words as "subjects or task names are strata", which is why it was his to decide. Public parts keep their strata (tasks, subjects).
2. **The small-sample rule now applies to every private part.** Line 435: "With fewer than 30 clusters: the cluster-robust standard error with a t quantile on (clusters − 1) degrees of freedom replaces the percentile bootstrap." Unchanged. Every private interval and bound of sections 9.1 and 10.2 to 10.4 is therefore a t interval on G − 1 degrees of freedom, **The confirmation step of line 460** ("2,000 simulations with the full percentile bootstrap inside") is a step of the power simulation of section 9.4, not an interval of section 9.2. For a private part the test M5 will use is the t rule, by line 435, so a confirmation with the percentile bootstrap inside would confirm a test M5 does not run. It is therefore run with the t rule inside: 2,000 simulations, the Monte Carlo standard error printed, both figures printed as line 460 says. This follows from line 435, but it changes what line 460's words describe, so it is listed as a departure (section 15, A16; review 2, F9).
3. **Sentences that assumed G = 300** are read at the G written: the M0 draw is "balanced over the G clusters" (line 388), 79 to 300 items per cluster at G of 19 to 5, instead of about 5 (line 457); the sealed pool is "spread evenly over the G clusters" (line 390), about 1,050 to 4,000 sealed items per cluster. How items are spread inside a cluster is section 10, part B.
4. **The interval of τ̂ below 30 clusters was not defined in ADR-0033 (review 1, R8); the method is now decided.** Line 457 prints τ̂ "with its cluster-bootstrap 90 % interval", and choice 18 (b) uses that interval's upper bound. Line 435 replaces the percentile bootstrap below 30 clusters but names no interval for a variance component. **Decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26, with choice 18 (b) (section 13 item 2): the χ² interval of the between-cluster variance, upper bound τ_up² = max(0, V·(G − 1)/χ²₀.₀₅,G₋₁ − s_w²·mean(1/m_g)), with V the variance of the cluster means on divisor G − 1.** It is the method the reviewers assumed and the one behind column (b) of section 6. It assumes roughly normal cluster means, which with 79 to 750 items per cluster (1,500 over 19 clusters down to 2) is reasonable for the means and untested for the paired differences of real models. The method joins the known-truth gate (section 12 item 6). The formula fixes the divisor of V at G − 1. **It does not fix the form of the standard error of a paired difference, CR1 (the sample standard deviation of the cluster means, divisor G − 1, over √G) or CR0 (divisor G), which ADR-0033 leaves open (review 3, G6).** At five clusters the form decides whether 0.02 is reachable at all, and it scales every private t interval (CR0 is narrower by the factor √((G − 1)/G): 0.71 at two clusters, 0.89 at five, 0.95 at ten). **The form is CR1, decided by the human lead on 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 19). It is the option unfavourable to passing.** It is registered with the plan at F, as the analysis rules of ADR-0033 are; it is no longer open, and F waits for no decision on it. **What this means for M0's printing (review 2, F10).** Line 457 says "Until it is decided M0 prints the floor both ways", and line 700 that for choices 15 to 19 "Nothing in M0 waits". Choice 18 is decided as (b), so line 457's "until it is decided" no longer applies to a private part: its floor is the upper-bound floor, and this text changes nothing else that line 457 prints. Draft 3's fallback, a line "upper-bound floor: NOT COMPUTED", is withdrawn: with the interval decided it has no case.
5. **A part with no, one or two clusters (review 1, R6).** Section 4 step 5, the bound and the capacity stage can each leave a part with very few clusters, or none (the worked example of section 4A ends with an empty part). With one cluster there is no standard error, no t quantile and no τ̂: no interval of sections 9 or 10 exists. With two clusters the t quantile on one degree of freedom is 6.31 (against 1.645 for a normal) and τ̂ rests on one degree of freedom. ADR-0033 has no rule for this. **There is no minimum number of clusters** (decided, section 13 item 13 (a)). **The reading, decided by the human lead on 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 7):** a part with no cluster has no pool, and every outcome of that part reads NOT EVALUABLE with the reason NO CLUSTER; a part with one cluster is printed with its accuracies only, and every outcome that needs an interval reads NOT EVALUABLE for that part with the reason ONE CLUSTER; a part with two or more clusters is computed as written and its degrees of freedom are printed beside every figure. At two clusters nothing on the grid is reachable under either form of the standard error (section 6).

## 10. The capacity gate and the pool builder

**Amended sentence** (section 6.2, line 273): "**Capacity gate (G-gen).** For every cluster and level, the number of distinct items the generator can produce is at least 10 times the number the pools demand. Otherwise engineering stop."

### Part A. The gate

1. **What "distinct items" means: reading (i), decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26 (section 13 item 1; lead ruling R-16 had left it to him).** ADR-0033 does not define it. Draft 1 wrote a structural definition (names, setting sentence and order of presentation erased) as if it were settled; that was lead ruling R-5 and is withdrawn from this text. The readings measured by the auditors were: (i) names relabelled only, with exact duplicates of the rendered question removed; (ii) labels and letters also relabelled; (iii) literal values that do not change the control flow or the solution path also discounted (under which 90 to 101 of 140 C1 cluster-levels fail). **(i) is the most favourable of the three to passing the capacity gate; it was named to him as such before he answered, and it was taken with failure counts under each reading in view.** The key of an item is its rendered question with names relabelled; two items with one key are one item. The key function is hashed with the generators at stage 0 and is the key of the capacity count (section 4A stage 4). The builder's own uniqueness test is a different thing and stays textual (part B item 6; review note N5).
2. **The demand is computed at the G of the map of record, by the worst-case reading (review 1, R1; lead rulings R-10, R-11, R-30).** The level mix of a part is chosen later (section 6.5) and may be a single level. The development pool and the sealed pool are each spread evenly over the clusters, so the largest cluster holds ⌈4,000/G⌉ + ⌈20,000/G⌉ of them. For a part with G clusters, **on each of the seven levels**:

   **D(G) = 10 × (⌈4,000 / G⌉ + ⌈20,000 / G⌉ + 100)** distinct items per cluster.

   4,000 is the development pool, 20,000 the sealed pool, 100 the tuning draw per level and part (section 8, lines 387 to 390). **The two reserve levels carry the full demand, not 10 × 100:** section 6.5 takes as candidates "every contiguous range of levels" and, if no range is interior, scores the reserve levels "and the rule is applied again", so a reserve level can carry the registered level mix and the pools with it. Taking the 100 tuning items whole for one cluster is more than the worst case (review note N8); it is kept because it is harmless and simpler. **G is that part's own count, G_e,A or G_e,B, in the current round of section 4A stage 4 (decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26, section 13 item 16 (a)).** Lead ruling R-11 took "the smaller part's cluster count" for both parts, and R-30, written after draft 3, restated it ("the smaller part decides: G = floor(surviving clusters / 2)"). This text takes each part's own, which for the larger part is **less strict** than R-11 and R-30 (review 2, F11). The reason: line 273 counts "the number the pools demand", and a part's pools are spread over that part's clusters, so the larger part's clusters are asked for fewer items. It relaxes the lead's own rulings, though no number of ADR-0033, and was named to the human lead as such before he answered. The working capacity figures of the narrow round were computed under R-30 and are not of record (part B).
3. **Values** (script part A): D(5) = 49,000; D(8) = 31,000; D(10) = 25,000; D(11) = 22,830; D(13) = 19,470; D(14) = 18,150; D(15) = 17,010; D(19) = 13,640. At the withdrawn G = 300 it was 1,810. A cluster must hold about seven and a half to twenty-seven times what it had to under the design as approved, on every one of seven levels. The sealed pool may be raised later, never lowered (line 409); a raise re-runs this gate.
4. **Per cluster and per lineage.** The gate is read on the cluster: the number of distinct items its registered lineages produce together at that level, counted on items built by the generators with the counting rule of section 4A stage 4 (each lineage and level on its own; the cluster's count is the union of its lineages' key sets) and not on a combinatorial argument. Because part B spreads a cluster's quota evenly over its registered lineages, each registered lineage of a cluster of k must also hold 10 times its own largest share: **D_lin(G, k) = 10 × (⌈⌈4,000/G⌉ / k⌉ + ⌈⌈20,000/G⌉ / k⌉ + ⌈100 / k⌉)**. For example D_lin(10, 2) = 12,500 and D_lin(13, 3) = 6,500. A lineage that cannot is NOT REGISTERED, and the gate is read again by the rounds of section 4A stage 4, with k and G as they then stand, until nothing more fails. The partition is not computed again. At the smallest counts the fixed point can reach, the demand is D(4) = 61,000, D(3) = 81,010, D(2) = 121,000 and D(1) = 241,000 (`addendum1_rev3_arithmetic.out.txt`).
5. **No cluster at some levels only.** A cluster that fails at any of the seven levels is not registered at all: registering it at some levels would make G differ by level, against the even spread of section 8 and the level rule of section 6.5. Lead ruling R-5 allowed "NOT REGISTERED at that level set"; this text refuses it and says so (the drafter's choice; conservative; reported to the human lead).
6. **The event is unchanged for the record of record:** a capacity failure there is an ENGINEERING STOP (section 10.7). Before the record, dropping a lineage is ordinary work under section 7 item 5, reported to the human lead with the list.

### Part B. The pool builder (the per-cluster builder; decided by the human lead, 2026-11-26, section 13 item 6 (a); it takes effect with the approval of this addendum)

**The builder as written does not do what this addendum needs.** `studies/m0/core.py` on `claude/m0-impl` (`generate_pool`, read at `9bcec484`): it takes the lineages of a part from the family partition, gives every **lineage** an equal quota (`allocate(size, [1.0] * len(lins))`), splits each lineage's quota over levels by the level mix, draws with `stream(seed, role, salt_label, eval_id, part, lin.id, str(lv))`, and rejects an item whose normalised text has been used (`text_hash`: NFKC, case-folded, whitespace collapsed), so two items that differ only in names are two items. `m0_draw` balances the 1,500 over lineages in the same way. With lineages merged into clusters this is no longer "balanced over the G clusters" (line 388) or "spread evenly over the G clusters" (line 390): on a made-up part of 7 clusters holding 3, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 lineages the largest cluster would get 30 % of every pool instead of one seventh (script part E), and the effective number of clusters would be below G.

**The builder.** For evaluation e, part p, a role, its seed, a size and a level mix, on the map of record. Wherever a count is divided "by largest remainder" below, it is by `allocate` as written at `9bcec484`: ties go to the earlier index in the stated order (review 3, G8).

1. **Clusters.** The global clusters assigned to p that have at least one registered lineage of e, in increasing code-point order of identity.
2. **Cluster quotas.** The size is divided equally over those clusters by largest remainder, ties to the cluster earlier in that order.
3. **Levels inside a cluster.** The cluster's quota is divided over the levels of the mix, in increasing level order, by largest remainder with the mix as weights.
4. **Lineages inside a cluster and level.** That count is divided equally over the cluster's registered lineages of e, in increasing order of id, by largest remainder. A lineage draws its shapes as its generator already does.
5. **Streams.** Unchanged: `stream(seed, role, salt_label, e, p, lineage id, level)`, with the derivation of section 4. A lineage's stream therefore does not depend on which cluster it sits in.
6. **Uniqueness and order.** Unchanged: an item is rejected if its normalised text is already used in any earlier role or earlier in this pool. The roles are built in the order of the rows of ADR-0033's table of splits (lines 387 to 390): the tuning draw, then the development pool, then the sealed pool, whose salted stream does not exist before F. Generation runs over clusters, then levels, then lineages, in the orders above, so the shared set of used texts is deterministic. A quota a lineage cannot fill is a capacity failure and is never moved to a sibling lineage or cluster. (The capacity count of section 4A stage 4 does not use this shared set; it counts each lineage on its own.)
7. **The M0 draw.** The 1,500 are divided equally over the part's clusters, then equally over each cluster's registered lineages, by largest remainder in the same orders; each lineage's items are sampled from its development items with `stream(400006, "m0-draw", lineage id)` as written.
8. **The record.** Every item carries its global cluster identity; pool hashes, counts "per family, cluster and level" (line 419) and every analysis use that field. The clusters of every interval and of the power simulation are these clusters, not lineages.

The third reviewer found this builder implementable and found that it meets lines 388 and 390: cluster quotas differ by at most one item and the M0 draw is equal per cluster (review 3, G8). One property he noted is left as it is: if the tuning draw of 100 per level is built with one call per level, each level's remainder goes to the same clusters, the earliest in the order (at three clusters the first gets 34 at every level). Changing that would need a rotation rule, which is a new choice; the counts per cluster and level are printed.

**Capacity is judged against a specified builder, never an assumed one (lead rulings R-22, R-28).** With items 1 and 6 decided, the capacity run of record is section 4A stage 4: the demand D(G) and D_lin(G, k) of part A, which follow from this builder's quotas; the key of part A item 1; and the counting rule of that stage. The runner's code is hashed at stage 0. No capacity run of record takes place before this addendum is approved (review 2, F2). The capacity figures produced so far, in the narrow round under R-28 and R-30, are working figures against `generate_pool` as written and at the smaller part's G; they are not of record. The lineages they left undecided (R-36, R-39) are decided at stage 4.

**The option not taken (item 6 b), kept for the record.** To keep `generate_pool` as written it would have had to change in one respect, which it cannot run without: a part's lineages are those whose global cluster section 4 assigned to that part (as written it takes them from `part_lineages`, which calls `family_partition`, the withdrawn split of families on the label `family-partition`). It would still give every lineage an equal quota, with a demand per lineage and level of D_w(L) = 10 × (⌈4,000 / L⌉ + ⌈20,000 / L⌉ + 100), where L is the number of lineages of e in the part; for example D_w(8) = 31,000, D_w(15) = 17,010, D_w(30) = 9,010 (`addendum1_rev3_arithmetic.out.txt`). A cluster of k lineages would then hold k / L of every pool and not 1 / G (for example 0.300 against 0.143), and lines 388 and 390 would not be met as worded.

Draft 1's sentence that items are spread "inside a lineage over its shapes" is withdrawn: a lineage's generator already chooses among its shapes, and this addendum adds no rule there.
