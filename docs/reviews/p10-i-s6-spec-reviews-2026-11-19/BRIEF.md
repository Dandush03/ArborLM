## The question

Does the flat memory's lead over the dense adapter on fresh data persist when both arms are trained until they have converged by a registered criterion (or to a much longer registered duration), on fresh draws? Three honest outcomes must be reachable and equally easy to reach: the lead PERSISTS at convergence; the dense adapter CATCHES UP (the lead falls inside the margin) or goes AHEAD; or convergence is NOT REACHED within the registered budget (then report the registered trend and claim nothing about the ordering). A negative result is as valuable as a positive one; the design must not make "persists" the default reading of an unfinished run.

## Design requirements (decide each from the code and records; give reasons; list what you could not decide)

1. **Enough fresh data.** Determine exactly why fresh data ends at 19,064 updates and what is needed to train to at least 4x and preferably 8x–16x the old duration without repeating data. If a larger corpus is needed, name candidates that are byte-level text of the same kind, with size, licence, source and checksum method; a download under 50 GB needs no further permission from the human lead (standing delegation) but must be recorded in the download ledger, and is an open choice if anything about it (licence, kind of text, size) is not routine. State how the new corpus is kept disjoint from everything earlier studies trained or evaluated on, and whether a change of corpus changes the question (then say how the old 16,384-update result is re-anchored on the new corpus: a replication point at 16,384 on the new data is probably required).
2. **A convergence rule that is a rule.** ADR-0026's "convergence rule" was a fixed duration with a tag. Here define a registered ladder of durations (for example doubling from 16,384) and a criterion per arm (improvement per interval at most ε, with ε and the interval reasoned), a registered maximum, and what is read where: the comparison at the first duration at which BOTH arms are converged, with the labels for "one converged, one not" and "neither" fixed in advance. Consider also the dense arm's best snapshot against the memory's (ADR-0024's dense-reading discussion): nothing may favour the memory.
3. **Fairness to the dense adapter.** Rates for both arms calibrated at the longer durations on calibration draws only, by code, with interior selections required (state the rule when a selection is at the edge: extend the grid before the registered run, never after); the dense arm's size and placement as ADR-0026 fixed them, plus a decision, with reasons, on whether a larger dense adapter (parameter- or compute-matched to the flat memory) must be an extra arm so that "ahead of dense" is not "ahead of a small dense". Say what "matched" means in bytes and in FLOPs from the code.
4. **Dependence for the flat memory.** P10-I-S3's line 1 (dependence) was read on the tree, and passed largely through harm (wrong values hurt in 27 of 27 cells). Define the same controls for the flat memory at the converged duration (values reassigned, batch-mean read, memory disabled = BASE bitwise), with the harm figures printed, so that a persisting lead is known to depend on stored content and not on added parameters.
