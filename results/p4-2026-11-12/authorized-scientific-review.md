Disposition: **useful frozen-backbone memory capacity and slot-association dependence established within the registered descriptive screen; dense-quality tolerance and composite P4 success not met.** Mean capacity gain C=D is 0.021988694591 nats/byte, above 0.005, with all 27 gains and all nine parent means positive. Mean memory loss improves from 2.378856565665 initially to 2.356867678325. Mean value-shuffling penalty P is 0.022221518611, above 0.005, with all 27 paired C/P values and all nine parent mean P values positive. These satisfy both capacity and association flags. All 27 memory cells individually exceed 0.005 for both effects; that descriptive observation is not an additional success gate.

Dense adapters improve every parent/seed pair more: mean dense loss is 2.350685040570 and mean dense gain is 0.028171332346. Mean Q=A-H is −0.006182637756, below the −0.005 tolerance; only 10/27 cells and 2/9 parent means meet tolerance, versus required 22/27 and 8/9. All 27 Q values are negative. The dense-quality flag, secondary memory-advantage flag and required three-flag composite are false. Retaining the positive capacity finding does not rename this failed composite as success.

Backbone freezing and exact disabled-baseline equality remove concurrent backbone optimization as an explanation for the gains. Disabling is the same numerical contrast as C, not an independent replication. Value permutation supports dependence on the trained slot associations, but does not identify semantic storage or prove that hierarchical routing is necessary. The fixed-tree retrieval mathematics were unchanged; this study contains no flat-memory comparison, learned topology restructuring, capacity scaling or physical I/O experiment. A dense addition with equal trainable parameters also helps and is stronger at this particular matched example/update budget. Matching parameter count and updates does not match active compute, initialization function, conditioning or optimal hyperparameters.

P4 is not a controlled comparison of freezing versus joint training. Its new memory initialization, training protocol and validation panel differ from P3, so it cannot identify the cause of P3’s weaker dependence or establish freezing as the superior optimization strategy.

The nine parents share three backbone initializations and three data orders, and all 27 comparisons share data and seed factors. They are not 27 independent backbone replications. The new panel has no exact overlap with prior panels or training candidates, but remains from the same corpus and may share articles or near-duplicates. Training repeats the same 2,048 chunks already seen by the backbone. The thresholds are descriptive design choices, not significance levels or population noninferiority tests. There is no broad proof of novelty, generalization, scaling, hierarchy-specific advantage or SSD usefulness. P5–P7 remain candidate phases requiring separate design and authorization; this review authorizes no additional experiment.
